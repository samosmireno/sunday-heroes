# The closing ballot counts

_2026-09-07._

Ratings on matches that have already been played have changed — 135 of them,
across 21 matches. Nobody's man of the match changed. Nothing was voted on
again: every ballot ever cast is still there, and these are the numbers those
same ballots always added up to.

## What was wrong

Voting on a match closes when the last player votes, and the ratings are worked
out in that moment. They were worked out from one ballot fewer than had been
cast: the closing player's own votes were written, and then the count that
should have included them was taken without them.

Both halves of the sum were off — the points a player received and the number of
votes they were measured against — so every rating on such a match was wrong,
not just the closing voter's. On a four-player match where everyone voted, the
squad was rated 1.33 / 2 / 1.67 / 1 where the twelve votes cast give
1.25 / 2.25 / 1.75 / 0.75.

The error is largest on small squads, where one ballot is a big share of the
vote. Matches whose voting ran out its deadline instead of closing on a last
ballot were always right.

## What moved

- **Ratings**, on the 21 matches that closed on their last ballot. Most move by
  a fraction; the largest single move is 0.18.
- **Nothing else, as it turns out.** The crown is worked out again from the
  corrected ratings, because a missing ballot can be the one that decides it and
  the player it was taken from would otherwise keep it forever. On these
  matches it never was: every man of the match still holds the top rating, and
  no career total moves.
- **A handful of career average ratings**, by a hundredth or two. One match had
  been counted as rated for the three players who received votes and as nothing
  at all for the seven who did not; those seven now hold the 0 they always
  showed on the match itself.

Where two players end up on the same top rating they are both man of the match.
That is not new, and the repair leaves a shared crown shared.

## Why the numbers were changed at all

A rating is visible history, and moving it is a surprising thing to do. It was
worth the surprise here because these were never a judgement anyone made: they
are a division by the wrong number, and every vote that went into them survives
to be divided correctly.

The rule that nothing is taken back still holds where it matters. No vote has
been discarded, no player has lost a vote they cast, and nothing about who may
vote has changed.
